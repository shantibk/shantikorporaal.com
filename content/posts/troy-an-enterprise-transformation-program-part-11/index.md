### Part 11: Three Leaders. One Crisis. Three Different Plans.

The Greek leadership team has convened another emergency meeting.

The Trojans have breached the defences, the fighting has reached the ships, and several senior commanders are injured.

The Greek delivery team is having what I suspect would be described in the quarterly report as *significant operational challenges*.

Which seems a little understated, given that people are actively dying.

Nestor has arrived after investigating the deteriorating situation.

He finds Agamemnon, Odysseus, and Diomedes.

All three are wounded.

And Agamemnon has a suggestion.

He would like to go home.

Again.

😂

At this point, I'm beginning to suspect the ships are less a transport solution and more his preferred coping mechanism.

But I actually have some sympathy for him.

He's injured. His army is struggling. The Trojans are pushing towards the ships, and the situation is getting worse.

He's watching a campaign that has already cost an extraordinary number of lives threaten to collapse completely.

Of course he wants a way out.

I think most of us would.

The problem is that wanting to escape a situation doesn't necessarily mean escaping is the best decision.

And Odysseus has some thoughts about that.

Quite a few, actually.

He points out that preparing the ships to leave while the fighting continues would send a fairly disastrous message to the soldiers.

Essentially, the leadership team would be announcing that they've lost confidence in the operation.

While everyone else is still trying to keep it alive.

Not ideal for morale.

I suspect Odysseus could benefit from some stakeholder communication training himself, but his concern is reasonable.

Agamemnon is looking at the immediate danger.

Odysseus is looking at what the proposed response would do to the people still fighting.

They're looking at the same crisis.

But they're not seeing exactly the same problem.

Then Diomedes offers a third perspective.

The commanders are wounded, so they can't simply charge back into battle.

But they can go out among the troops.

They can be visible. Encourage the soldiers. Help restore some confidence.

They don't have to fight to make a difference.

Which, considering the circumstances, is a remarkably practical suggestion.

Although I suspect the workplace safety adviser would have several questions about sending three injured executives towards an active battlefield.

😂

And Agamemnon listens.

He accepts Diomedes' proposal.

Which I think is worth noticing.

Because this isn't a meeting where one person arrives with the correct answer, and everyone else is simply being difficult.

Nestor has noticed that the situation has changed and helps the others reassess it.

Agamemnon sees the danger of staying.

Odysseus sees the danger of leaving.

Diomedes sees something they can still do.

Each perspective adds information that the others need.

And none of them has to be entirely wrong for the group to reach a better decision.

That's the part I find interesting.

When we're under pressure, it's easy to assume that the problem we can see most clearly must be the problem everyone should focus on.

But our view is shaped by what we're responsible for, what we've experienced, what we're afraid of, and what we're trying to protect.

Sometimes someone else's disagreement isn't a rejection of our concerns.

They're seeing a consequence we haven't considered.

And sometimes the most useful contribution isn't another argument about which option is worse.

It's finding a third option that moves everyone forward.

I also keep coming back to Agamemnon.

Because there's something very human about wanting to abandon a situation that's become frightening, exhausting, and overwhelming.

Recognising that feeling doesn't make him a bad leader.

But it doesn't automatically make his proposed response the right one either.

Understanding why someone wants to leave isn't the same as agreeing that leaving is the best decision.

And when people are depending on you, the decision has consequences beyond your own relief.

The Greeks are still in trouble.

The commanders are still injured.

And the battlefield is still an extremely questionable location for a leadership walkabout.

But at least they've managed to consider different perspectives and agree on a practical next step.

Without anyone threatening to murder the person offering advice.

Which, after the last few chapters, feels like genuine progress for the Greek and Trojan leadership development programme.

😂
