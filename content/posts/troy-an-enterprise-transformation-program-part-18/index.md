### The Final Governance Meeting

Well, we've reached the end of Homer's *Iliad*.

24 books.

Ten years of war.

Countless meetings that should have been emails.

And after all that?

**TROY IS STILL STANDING.**

🤣

The project hasn't been delivered.

Helen hasn't been recovered.

And our governance team is preparing to close out this phase of the program.

But before they go, there's one final stakeholder meeting.

Priam, the elderly King of Troy, enters the Greek camp.

He's come to see Achilles.

The man who killed his son, Hector.

And who has continued mistreating Hector's body even after his death.

Priam wants to take his son home.

Now, imagine that meeting.

The most dangerous warrior in the Greek army.

And an elderly father asking him for compassion.

**THIS IS NOT A STANDARD STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT.**

😂

Priam doesn't threaten Achilles.

Instead, he asks him to think about his own father.

And something changes.

Achilles remembers his father and Patroclus.

Both men begin to cry.

Two enemies, united by grief.

After all the fighting and revenge, it's this moment of shared humanity that finally reaches Achilles.

And I think there's something important in that.

We've all encountered difficult colleagues, frustrated customers, or stakeholders who seem determined to make everything harder.

Sometimes we become so focused on the disagreement that we stop seeing the person on the other side.

We defend our position.

We rehearse our arguments.

**THE MEETING HAS BECOME A BATTLEFIELD.**

😂

But what happens when we stop trying to win and start trying to understand?

Not necessarily to agree.

Just to understand.

Because understanding can create possibilities that arguing couldn't.

Priam doesn't convince Achilles that the war was wrong.

But Achilles agrees to return Hector's body and allows time for the Trojans to bury him.

**THE FIRST PRODUCTIVE STAKEHOLDER MEETING IN THE ENTIRE TROY PROGRAM.**

🤣

And it didn't happen because someone had the strongest argument.

It happened because two enemies recognised something they shared.

Their humanity.

So that's where Homer leaves us.

Not with a conquered city.

Not with a completed project.

But with Hector's funeral.

**FINAL GOVERNANCE REPORT:**

**PROJECT DELIVERED:** No.

**TROY CAPTURED:** No.

**STAKEHOLDER RELATIONSHIPS:** Surprisingly improved.

**LESSONS LEARNED:** Sometimes the breakthrough isn't finding a better way to win.

**IT'S REMEMBERING THERE'S A PERSON ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THE TABLE.**

❤️

**END OF ILIAD PHASE.**

**PROJECT STATUS: STILL ONGOING.**

🤣
