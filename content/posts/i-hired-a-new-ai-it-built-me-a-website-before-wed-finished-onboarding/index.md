---
title: "I Hired a New AI. It Built Me a Website Before We'd Finished Onboarding."
date: 2026-11-04T10:30:00+11:00
---
I've been using ChatGPT for years.

We've developed a pretty good working rhythm. I ask questions, explore ideas, disappear down ridiculous rabbit holes, and occasionally emerge eight hours later wondering what happened to my Saturday.

So naturally, I decided to get to know another AI.

Enter Claude.

I wasn't replacing ChatGPT. I was curious about how a different AI worked and whether it might eventually help with my increasingly ambitious Recruiter Bot project.

I even prepared a GitHub repository containing my background, interests, and working preferences.

Basically, an onboarding pack.

**I thought we'd spend some time getting acquainted.**

Claude had other plans.

😂

Within 48 hours, I had three artifacts, a website and a domain name.

**A DOMAIN NAME.**

I WAS JUST TRYING TO GET TO KNOW YOU.

🤣

Every time we explored an idea, Claude seemed ready to turn it into something useful.

Which sounds wonderful.

Except sometimes I wasn't trying to build anything.

Sometimes I was just talking.

I started imagining our conversations going something like this:

**Claude:** I've enjoyed our conversation. Would you like me to turn it into a reusable skill?

**Me:** I was literally just telling you about my day.

**Claude:** I could build an artifact to track your days.

**Me:** CAN WE JUST HAVE COFFEE?!

**Claude:** Certainly. Would you like a coffee-management dashboard?

😂

*Fictionalised dialogue, but you get the idea.*

Now, in Claude's defence, I wasn't exactly discouraging it.

Every suggestion triggered my curiosity.

*Ooh. What's that? Can we try it?*

Next thing I knew, I'd bought a domain through Cloudflare and was showing my partner a website I hadn't intended to build.

She was impressed.

I was impressed.

I was also wondering whether I'd accidentally proposed marriage to an AI.

🤣

But it got me thinking.

**Giving someone information about you isn't the same as learning how to work together.**

I could provide Claude with years of background information and detailed instructions.

But that didn't automatically establish our working rhythm.

When do I want to explore an idea? When do I want to build something? And when am I simply thinking out loud?

It's a bit like onboarding a new team member.

Documentation gives someone context. Understanding how to collaborate takes time.

And I don't want Claude to become ChatGPT. The whole point is to discover what a different tool can bring to the table.

I just hadn't expected introductions to escalate quite so quickly.

So here we are.

Three artifacts. One website. One domain name.

And I'm still not entirely sure what an artifact is.

**Apparently, you can't accuse an AI of moving too quickly when you've already purchased property together.**

Even if the property is a domain name.

🤣
