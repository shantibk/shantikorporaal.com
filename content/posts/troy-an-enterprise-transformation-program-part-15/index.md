### When It Gets Personal

Well.

Management finally got what they wanted.

Achilles is coming back to work.

After refusing to participate, ignoring management's increasingly desperate requests, and watching the project fall apart...

**THE STAR PERFORMER IS BACK!**

🎉

There's just one small problem.

He's absolutely furious.

And nobody has any control over him.

🤣

Welcome back to Troy.

In Book 18 of *The Iliad*, Achilles learns that Patroclus is dead.

He's devastated.

And his grief quickly turns to rage.

Suddenly, his long-running dispute with Agamemnon isn't quite so important anymore.

Which is interesting.

Because management has tried everything to get Achilles back.

Appeals to duty.

Desperate requests.

Even a rather impressive compensation package.

Nothing worked.

But Patroclus' death?

That changed everything.

**AGAMEMNON: So, we've resolved our disagreement?**

**ACHILLES: NO. I'VE JUST GOT BIGGER PROBLEMS.**

🤣

And that's what struck me.

Achilles hasn't necessarily forgiven Agamemnon.

He's simply found something that matters more.

The grievance that consumed him has been pushed aside by something deeply personal.

Of course, his new focus is revenge.

So we're not exactly witnessing a masterclass in emotional regulation.

😂

Meanwhile, there's an operational problem.

Hector has taken Achilles' armour.

And apparently, sending your most powerful warrior back into battle requires equipment.

Fortunately, Achilles' mother, Thetis, has organised emergency procurement.

She's gone directly to Hephaestus, the divine blacksmith.

**PROCUREMENT REQUEST: URGENT.**

**SUPPLIER: HEPHAESTUS.**

**SPECIFICATION: DIVINE.**

**BUDGET: DON'T ASK.**

🤣

And this is where Homer absolutely cracked me up.

The description of the armour being made is EXTRAORDINARY.

Especially the shield.

Cities. Farming. Weddings. Battles. Dancing.

An entire world depicted in metal.

I was genuinely laughing at how much detail we got about this armour compared with the construction of the Greeks' entire bloody defensive wall.

**THE WALL GOT A CONSTRUCTION UPDATE.**

**THE ARMOUR GOT A DOCUMENTARY SERIES.**

😂

By Book 19, the armour has arrived.

Achilles and Agamemnon publicly put their dispute aside.

Management has its star performer back.

**PROJECT RECOVERY PLAN: BACK ON TRACK.**

Well...

Not quite.

Because Achilles is so consumed by grief and anger that he wants to charge straight into battle.

Odysseus suggests the soldiers should probably eat first.

You know.

Basic workforce management.

**OPERATIONS: We should feed the troops before deployment.**

**ACHILLES: NO TIME.**

**OPERATIONS: They've been fighting all day.**

**ACHILLES: I HAVE A HECTOR TO FIND.**

🤣

And there it is.

Management finally has its motivated employee.

Unfortunately, his motivation has absolutely nothing to do with management's objectives.

Which got me thinking.

Sometimes we can become so consumed by a grievance that it takes over our attention.

Until something else happens.

Something that matters more.

And suddenly, the argument we couldn't let go of isn't our biggest concern anymore.

But Achilles also reminds us that **finding a new focus doesn't automatically mean finding a better one.**

His anger at Agamemnon has been replaced by something far more powerful.

And far more dangerous.

So perhaps the question isn't just what motivates us.

**It's whether that motivation is taking us somewhere we actually want to go.**
